# Foreigners Hub — Design Direction

## Three stylistic approaches

### Theme Name: New City, Sorted
Very bright editorial energy for people building a life somewhere new; practical information is presented with the confidence of a good city guide.
Probability: 0.07

### Theme Name: Soft Landing
A quieter hospitality-led direction with warm neutrals, calm photography, and a reassuring rental concierge tone.
Probability: 0.03

### Theme Name: Route & Roof
A punchier wayfinding identity built around transport lines, map marks, and clear modular service blocks.
Probability: 0.09

## Chosen approach: New City, Sorted

### Design Movement
Contemporary editorial utility: the clarity of a modern city guide combined with the optimism of a youth mobility brand.

### Core Principles
1. **Momentum over friction:** every section should move visitors toward a decision without making them decode the interface.
2. **Useful can still feel expressive:** bold display type, directional lines, and vivid category accents make practical information memorable.
3. **Trust through specifics:** real prices, inclusions, terms, and a visible online process replace vague promises.
4. **Lightness with structure:** the canvas stays airy and approachable while cards, rails, and alignment cues keep the story grounded.

### Color Philosophy
Use the supplied cobalt blue (#315CFF) as the recognizable anchor and action color. Green belongs to bikes and successful outcomes; orange belongs to apartments and attention states. The background stays near-white (#fbfcff / #f7f9fc) so information feels clean and premium rather than sterile. Red remains reserved for critical states and does not appear decoratively.

### Layout Paradigm
A left-to-right journey map rather than a centered marketing stack: a floating navigation rail, asymmetric hero with image and copy, offset service cards, a price rail, and a process line that visually carries visitors from browse to move-in.

### Signature Elements
- A cobalt route line with circular waypoints used as a visual thread across process and pricing sections.
- Oversized italicized display headlines that feel like a confident city guide.
- Small “field note” labels and category chips that make the page feel collected from real operations, not generated from generic marketing blocks.

### Interaction Philosophy
Interactions should feel like checking a helpful rental dashboard: decisive and fast, with visible state changes. Buttons compress on press, pricing updates immediately when a plan changes, and forms respond with clear confirmation rather than mystery.

### Animation
Use short ease-out transforms and opacity transitions under 300ms. Hero elements enter in a staggered cascade; route points drift only a few pixels on hover; service cards lift subtly; selected plan cards shift from neutral to cobalt with a quick snap. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` by disabling entrance and floating animations.

### Typography System
Display: Space Grotesk, 700–800 weight, used for expressive headlines and prices. Body: DM Sans, 400–600 weight, used for navigation, labels, paragraphs, and controls. Use generous line-height for body copy and tight tracking only for metadata labels.

### Brand Essence
Foreigners Hub is the practical first move for students and young foreigners settling into a new city: move around, find a place, and keep going without the usual friction.
Personality: **direct, upbeat, trustworthy**.

### Brand Voice
Headlines are confident and human, never corporate. CTAs are specific and active. Microcopy explains the next step instead of hyping the product.

Example lines:
- “The basics of a new city, sorted.”
- “Choose a plan. We’ll keep you moving.”

### Wordmark & Logo
Use a compact route-and-door mark beside the wordmark. The mark should suggest both a bike wheel arc and a doorway, reinforcing the two-service promise without spelling it out.

### Signature Brand Color
**Foreigners Hub cobalt — #315CFF**. It is the visual signal for movement, clarity, and action across every important touchpoint.
